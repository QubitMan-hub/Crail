# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, weighted equally:

- **Reel viewers.** People who follow @qubitman_ftw on Instagram and tap through from a daily Reel, mostly on phones inside Instagram's in-app browser. They arrive curious about the one feature the Reel showed, play for a minute or two, and come back for the next day.
- **Future PC players.** CRAIL is meant to reach Steam one day, so a longer, keyboard-and-mouse session on a desktop browser has to hold up too.

## Product Purpose

CRAIL is a game built in public, one day at a time: every day, the most-liked comment on the latest Reel decides the next feature, and it gets built for real. The player rolls a red cube around an isometric world that keeps filling up with what the comments asked for (goths, femboys, a dictator and his army, a cube cult, an octopus thief and his squid friend, rival fighters, a park, trees). Success is people coming back each day to see their idea in the game, and commenting the next one.

## Positioning

Every feature can be traced to a real person's comment, credited in the game with their handle and their exact words, typos included. Nobody else's game is shaped by this audience, this way.

## Operating Context

- Daily loop: Reel posted, comments collected, the top comment built, the next Reel shows it.
- Hosted as a static site on GitHub Pages; three.js, TypeScript, Vite; no server.
- Played mostly on phones (touch: tap to move, pinch to zoom) and on desktop (WASD or arrows, scroll to zoom, M for the map).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Copyrighted characters are only ever built as original homages (Sonny and Prinz, not Goku and Vegeta; an off-brand Picachu).
- Comments about real people and politics are not built.
- Progress kept per player in the browser only (zoom, sound, blocks rolled); there is no shared server.
- Must run in Instagram's in-app browser on mid-range phones.

## Brand Commitments

- The name CRAIL and its wordmark.
- The player is the red cube; the world is made of soft blocks.
- Comment credits: every feature shows who asked for it and their exact words.
- Deadpan humour: short, dry, funny lines in the game.
- The creator delegated everything else ("do whatever you think").
